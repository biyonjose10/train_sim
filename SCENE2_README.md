# Train Sim — scenes 1 and 2

Two playable chapters that run into each other:

**Scene 1 — `Assets/Scenes/SampleScene.unity`.** The train runs in towards the station. An
approach signal drops to red behind you as you pass it. Press **space** to brake, and the train
stops at the platform in front of the red station signal.

**Scene 2 — `Assets/Scenes/Scene2_StationBoarding.unity`.** Loads automatically once the train has
settled. Passengers board from the platform while the camera cuts through three shots. At **9.0 s**
it cuts back to the train with the signal in frame; at **10.0 s** the signal turns green and the
train is yours to move. That is where scene 2 ends.

## How the scenes are made

Both are generated by **`Tools/generate_scenes.py`**, and both are built *from his
`SampleScene.unity`*. That matters: the lighting, skybox, rails, directional light and signal are
his by construction rather than by being copied by hand, so they cannot drift apart.

An earlier version of scene 2 was built on the asset pack demo scene
(`environment with station.unity`) instead. That scene has `m_Fog: 1` and a skybox reference
pointing at a GUID that is not in the project, which is what produced the foggy purple background.
Both scenes now carry his settings: **`m_Fog: 0`** and the built-in Default-Skybox
(`fileID: 10304`), the plain blue sky.

The station and the ground are lifted out of the pack scene as **literal YAML blocks** and
re-anchored — the 31 platform, shelter, bench and vending-machine instances plus the `stage.asset`
terrain at `(-101, 0, -102.1)` — so their layout is exactly what the pack author placed, in both
scenes at identical coordinates.

```
python Tools/generate_scenes.py      # rebuilds both scenes
python Tools/validate_scene.py Assets/Scenes/SampleScene.unity
```

The generator always reads his scene from **git** (`origin/main`), never from the file on disk, so
re-running it can never layer changes on top of a scene it already modified.

`Tools/validate_scene.py` checks a scene structurally: every local fileID resolves, parent and
child transforms agree in both directions, GameObjects and their components point at each other,
and SceneRoots matches the actual roots. It passes on the untouched pack scene as a control.

## Layout numbers

All at the top of `Tools/generate_scenes.py`:

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Train start | `z = -95` — a short run-in that stays on the terrain |
| Train stop | `z = -44.46` — **exactly where he had parked it**, nose 2 units short of the signal |
| Station signal | `(-5.9773, 3.0711, 18.459)` scale `1.3712` — his, untouched |
| Approach signal | `z = -70`, midway along the run-in |
| Boarding platform | west, `x ≈ -6.9` — the train's left as it faces the signal |
| Passenger feet | `y = 2.12` |

His train position became the **stop waypoint**, which is why the stop lands where he intended.
The train is 122 units long (probe: `2.66 x 4.65 x 122.16`), so at `z = -44.46` its nose sits at
`z = 16.45`.

## What was missing from scene 1

It did nothing on Play. All of this is now fixed:

- **No scripts were attached to anything.** `TrainStationButtonStop` and `Signal1Trigger` existed
  as files but were on no GameObject.
- **`TagManager.asset` had `tags: []`** — the `Train` tag his `Signal1Trigger` compares against
  did not exist, so it could never have fired. The tag is now defined and set on the train.
- **There were no trigger colliders at all** (every collider had `isTrigger: 0`), and the train
  had no collider or rigidbody. It now has a box collider and a kinematic rigidbody, which is what
  makes `OnTriggerEnter` fire at the approach signal.
- **The Main Camera was still at Unity's default** `(0, 1, -10)`. It now carries
  `TrainFollowCamera` and trails the train.
- **The three signal spheres all used the built-in default material.** They are now red / yellow /
  green, using his `New Material.mat` and `New Material 3.mat` plus a new `Signal Green.mat` —
  the project had red and yellow but no green.
- **There was no stop waypoint**, no ground and no station.

## Who owns the ten seconds

**Scene 2 does.** Scene 1 stops the train and hands over immediately, so you never wait twice.

Scene 1 attaches his `TrainStationButtonStop` but deliberately leaves its `lastSignalRedLight` and
`lastSignalGreenLight` **empty** — the script null-checks both, so nothing turns green there. The
red lamp is simply active in the scene. `ArriveAtStationLoader` then loads scene 2, watching
distance and speed rather than a trigger so it cannot fail on a missing tag.

## Passengers

`Hitogatas.fbx` imports as a Humanoid rig with a full skeleton but **no animation clips**, and
there is no `.anim` or `.controller` anywhere in the project. So `PassengerWalker.cs` drives the
walk itself: it finds `thigh_stretch.l/r` and `arm_stretch.l/r` by name and swings them with a
sine wave, with a small bob on `spine_01.x`. If they swing the wrong way, flip `Swing Axis` on the
component; if the bones cannot be found it logs a warning and they walk without swinging.

The train mesh has no door meshes and no interior, so boarding is staged from outside: each figure
walks to a queue point, then to a door point at `x = -3.0`, which is inside the carriage skin, so
the body hides them as they disappear.

## Editor menu — Tools > Train Sim

- **Open Scene 1** / **Open Scene 2** — jump straight to a scene.
- **Open Both Scenes** — loads scene 2 additively alongside scene 1, with scene 1 active so Play
  still starts the run in.
- **Add Scenes To Build Settings** — both scenes must be listed or neither will load at runtime.
- **Probe Layout** — prints the real mesh bounds and the terrain extent. Run it if anything looks
  like it is floating or sunk, then fix the constants in the generator rather than dragging things
  around, or the next regeneration will undo your work.

## Unity version

The project targets **6000.4.6f1**. Opening it in an older editor rewrites `ProjectVersion.txt`
and fails to resolve `com.unity.xr.arcore`, `com.unity.xr.arfoundation`,
`com.unity.modules.adaptiveperformance` and `com.unity.modules.vectorgraphics`, which do not exist
in Unity 6.0.

## Still not fixed
